## Ⅰ. Complete revised English draft

### 2.1 Construction of the Butyrate Biosynthetic Pathway

Starting with the butyrate-producing strain *Bacillus megaterium* But-0, a series of chromosomal modifications was introduced using CRISPR-Cas9-mediated homologous recombination. Genes associated with competing by-product pathways were sequentially deleted, and heterologous butyrate biosynthetic genes were integrated at the corresponding loci to reduce by-product formation and enhance butyrate production (Fig. 1A).

The lactate-forming branch was targeted first. Deletion of the lactate dehydrogenase gene *ldhA*, together with integration of the *Escherichia coli* acetyl-CoA acetyltransferase gene *atoB* at the same locus, generated But-1. Its butyrate titer reached 11.63 g/L, an increase of 10.44% over But-0 (10.53 g/L), while lactate concentration decreased from 4.30 to 1.03 g/L (75.97%; Fig. 1B). OD₆₀₀ decreased from 14.97 to 12.37 (17.37%). Nevertheless, butyrate accounted for a greater proportion of the measured organic acids on a mass basis, increasing from 41.7% in But-0 to 47.7% in But-1 (Fig. 1C). Acetate and succinate remained at 5.23 and 6.47 g/L, respectively, indicating that substantial accumulation of competing by-products persisted.

To address acetate accumulation, the *ackA* gene encoding acetate kinase was deleted from But-1 and replaced at the same chromosomal locus with the *hbd* gene encoding 3-hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydrogenase from *Clostridium acetobutylicum*. The resulting strain, But-2, reached a butyrate titer of 12.67 g/L, 8.88% higher than that of its parent. In contrast, acetate concentration fell from 5.23 to 3.50 g/L (33.12%), increasing the butyrate-to-acetate mass concentration ratio (B/A) from 2.22 to 3.62 (Fig. 1B). Lactate and succinate concentrations also declined, from 1.03 to 0.60 g/L and from 6.47 to 4.73 g/L, respectively. The butyrate mass fraction among the measured organic acids increased to 58.9% (Fig. 1C), whereas OD₆₀₀ rose from 12.37 to 13.77 (11.32%; Fig. 1B). Thus, the combined modification was associated with higher butyrate accumulation and lower concentrations of the measured competing organic acids.

The subsequent modification aimed to limit ethanol formation while extending the butyrate pathway. The *adhE* gene encoding aldehyde/alcohol dehydrogenase was deleted from But-2, and the *C. acetobutylicum crt* gene encoding 3-hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydratase was integrated at this locus to obtain But-3 (Fig. 1A). Butyrate titer increased only marginally, from 12.67 to 12.73 g/L (0.53%), despite a decrease in acetate concentration from 3.50 to 2.83 g/L (19.05%; Fig. 1B). Lactate and succinate, however, rose from 0.60 to 0.97 g/L and from 4.73 to 5.17 g/L, respectively. While OD₆₀₀ increased from 13.77 to 15.17 (10.17%), the butyrate mass fraction remained essentially unchanged (58.7% in But-3 versus 58.9% in But-2; Fig. 1B,C). The increase in OD₆₀₀ was therefore not accompanied by a comparable improvement in butyrate accumulation or its share of the measured organic acids.

For the next step, the pyruvate formate-lyase gene *pflB* was deleted from But-3 to target formate formation, and the *ter* gene encoding trans-2-enoyl-CoA reductase from *C. acetobutylicum* was integrated at the same locus to generate But-4 (Fig. 1A). Butyrate titer increased from 12.73 to 13.53 g/L (6.28%), and lactate and acetate concentrations decreased to 0.77 and 2.50 g/L, respectively (Fig. 1B). In contrast, succinate concentration rose from 5.17 to 6.77 g/L (30.97%), reaching the highest level among But-0 through But-5. This increase coincided with a decline in the butyrate mass fraction from 58.7% to 57.4% (Fig. 1C). OD₆₀₀ also decreased from 15.17 to 14.13 (6.81%; Fig. 1B). The accumulation of succinate despite a higher butyrate titer provided a clear basis for targeting the succinate-forming branch in the following round.

Accordingly, the fumarate reductase gene cluster *frdABCD* was deleted from But-4, and the *E. coli* acyl-CoA thioesterase II gene *tesB* was integrated at the same locus, yielding But-5 (Fig. 1A). Butyrate titer rose from 13.53 to 17.23 g/L (27.34%), whereas succinate concentration decreased sharply from 6.77 to 2.43 g/L (64.04%; Fig. 1B). Lactate and acetate concentrations further decreased to 0.57 and 2.10 g/L, respectively, with only a small change in OD₆₀₀ (14.13 to 14.43; 2.12%). By the fifth engineering step, the summed concentration of the measured by-products had decreased from 14.73 g/L in But-0 to 5.10 g/L in But-5, a reduction of 65.38% (Fig. 1D). Meanwhile, the butyrate mass fraction increased from 57.4% in But-4 to 77.2% in But-5, exceeding the starting strain But-0 (41.7%) by 35.5 percentage points (Fig. 1C). The greater butyrate titer and markedly lower succinate and total by-product concentrations were achieved without a substantial change in OD₆₀₀ relative to But-4.

Five further deletion-and-integration modifications were introduced sequentially into But-5, retaining its previous modifications while targeting genes associated with acetate formation, alanine metabolism, oxaloacetate replenishment, and the methylglyoxal branch (Fig. 1A). The *poxB*, *pta*, *alaT*, *ppc*, and *mgsA* genes were deleted, with *atoB* from *E. coli*, *hbd* and *crt* from *C. acetobutylicum*, *ter* from *Treponema denticola*, and *tesB* from *E. coli* integrated at the respective loci. The additional *T. denticola ter* gene at the *ppc* locus, alongside the *C. acetobutylicum ter* gene previously integrated at the *pflB* locus, was intended to further strengthen crotonyl-CoA reduction. The resulting strain, But-6, produced 22.07 g/L butyrate, increases of 28.05% over But-5 (17.23 g/L) and 109.49% over But-0 (10.53 g/L; Fig. 1B). Although OD₆₀₀ declined from 14.43 to 13.10 (9.24%), But-6 exhibited the highest butyrate titer among the engineered strains. It was therefore selected as the parent for three successive rounds of mutagenesis: ARTP, UV, and ARTP.
