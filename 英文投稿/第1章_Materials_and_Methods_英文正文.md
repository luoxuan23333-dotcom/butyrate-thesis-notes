# 1 Materials and Methods

## 1.1 Materials

### 1.1.1 Bacterial strains

*Bacillus megaterium* CCTCC M2021167, obtained from the China Center for Type Culture Collection (CCTCC), was used as the starting strain and designated But-0. A series of engineered strains, But-1 through But-6, was constructed by successive deletions of genes associated with competing metabolic pathways and chromosomal integration of heterologous butyrate biosynthetic genes. The engineered strain But-6 was subsequently subjected to three successive rounds of atmospheric and room-temperature plasma (ARTP), ultraviolet (UV), and ARTP mutagenesis, yielding the selected mutants But-6A100, But-6B100, and But-6C100, respectively. The strains used in this study are listed in Table 1.

### 1.1.2 Main reagents and equipment

Restriction endonucleases, PrimeSTAR high-fidelity DNA polymerase, and Taq DNA polymerase were purchased from TaKaRa Bio (Dalian, China). The one-step homologous recombination kit was obtained from Nanjing Jujiang Biotechnology (Nanjing, China). Plasmid extraction, DNA gel extraction, and PCR product purification kits were purchased from Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China), and the bacterial genomic DNA extraction kit was obtained from Tiangen Biotech (Beijing, China). Heterologous gene synthesis and PCR primer synthesis were performed by Yixin Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Chromatographic standards of butyric, lactic, acetic, and succinic acids were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, and the remaining biochemical and inorganic reagents were obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd.

The main instruments included a PCR thermal cycler, electroporator, gel imaging system, electrophoresis apparatus, and refrigerated high-speed benchtop centrifuge (Eppendorf, Germany); a visible-light spectrophotometer (Shanghai Yidian Analytical Instruments Co., Ltd., China); an SBA-40D biosensor analyzer (Biology Institute, Shandong Academy of Sciences, China); a high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA); an analytical balance and precision pH meter (Mettler-Toledo, Switzerland); a 5 L mechanically stirred bioreactor (Dibier Bioengineering (Shanghai) Co., Ltd., China); an ARTP mutagenesis apparatus (Wuxi Yuanqing Tianmu Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China); and a UV mutagenesis apparatus.

## 1.2 Culture Media and Cultivation Conditions

### 1.2.1 Seed media

The solid medium used for strain activation contained (g/L): glucose, 30.0; yeast extract, 5.0; peptone, 5.0; and agar, 15.0. The liquid seed medium contained (g/L): glucose, 30.0; yeast extract, 5.0; and peptone, 5.0. Both media were used at their natural pH and autoclaved at 121 °C for 20 min. Glucose was prepared and sterilized separately and aseptically mixed with the other components after cooling, immediately before inoculation.

Luria–Bertani (LB) medium used for plasmid construction and preparation of cell suspensions for mutagenesis contained (g/L): peptone, 10.0; yeast extract, 5.0; and NaCl, 10.0. For solid LB medium, agar was added at 15.0 g/L.

### 1.2.2 Fermentation medium

The basal liquid fermentation medium contained (g/L): glucose, 30.0; yeast extract, 5.0; and peptone, 5.0. The medium was used at its natural pH. Glucose was sterilized separately and aseptically combined with the other medium components before use. The sterilization conditions were 121 °C for 20 min.

### 1.2.3 Seed cultivation

Stock cultures were streaked onto solid activation medium and incubated at 30 °C for 24 h. A single colony was transferred into a 100 mL Erlenmeyer flask containing 25 mL of liquid seed medium and cultured at 30 °C and 200 rpm for 16 h until the logarithmic growth phase. The resulting culture was used as the inoculum.

### 1.2.4 Shake-flask fermentation

Seed culture was inoculated at 1% (v/v) into a 500 mL Erlenmeyer flask containing 100 mL of fermentation medium and cultivated at 30 °C and 200 rpm for 72 h. At the end of fermentation, optical density at 600 nm (OD₆₀₀) and the concentrations of butyrate, lactate, acetate, and succinate in the fermentation broth were determined.

## 1.3 Construction of Engineered Strains

### 1.3.1 Preparation of homologous recombination donor DNA

Homologous recombination donor DNA fragments were prepared for the target loci and heterologous genes listed in Table 1. Construction of But-1 is described as an example. Using genomic DNA from the starting strain But-0 as the template, the upstream and downstream homology arms flanking the *ldhA* locus were amplified by PCR. The *E. coli*-derived *atoB* gene was amplified from a template carrying *atoB*^{Ec}. The target gene was assembled with expression regulatory elements, including a strong promoter and terminator. The upstream homology arm, the *atoB*^{Ec} expression cassette, and the downstream homology arm were subsequently joined by overlap-extension PCR (fusion PCR) to generate the complete donor DNA fragment. Donor DNA fragments for the other target loci were prepared using the same approach.

### 1.3.2 Sequential construction of engineered strains

Chromosomal modifications were performed using CRISPR-Cas9-mediated homologous recombination. The homologous recombination donor DNA, the pTargetF plasmid carrying an sgRNA specific to the target locus, and the pCas plasmid were introduced into competent host cells by electroporation to achieve the intended chromosomal modifications.

Starting from But-0, the *ldhA* locus was deleted and replaced with *atoB*^{Ec} to generate But-1. The *ackA* locus was then modified by integration of *hbd*^{Ca} into But-1, yielding But-2. Subsequent deletion-and-integration steps, $\Delta adhE::crt^{\mathrm{Ca}}$, $\Delta pflB::ter^{\mathrm{Ca}}$, and $\Delta frdABCD::tesB^{\mathrm{Ec}}$, generated But-3, But-4, and But-5, respectively. Finally, five further modifications, $\Delta poxB::atoB^{\mathrm{Ec}}$, $\Delta pta::hbd^{\mathrm{Ca}}$, $\Delta alaT::crt^{\mathrm{Ca}}$, $\Delta ppc::ter^{\mathrm{Td}}$, and $\Delta mgsA::tesB^{\mathrm{Ec}}$, were introduced sequentially into But-5 to obtain But-6.

### 1.3.3 Screening and verification of recombinant strains

After electroporation and recovery, cells were spread onto selective agar plates containing the appropriate antibiotics. Single colonies were picked as candidate transformants and screened by colony PCR using verification primers located outside the homology arms of each target locus. The expected chromosomal deletions and heterologous gene integrations were verified by agarose gel electrophoresis and DNA sequencing. Confirmed positive clones were subjected to induction and cultivation at elevated temperature to remove the editing plasmids pTargetF and pCas. After the target sequences were checked again, the verified strains were preserved as glycerol stocks for subsequent rounds of genome engineering.

## 1.4 Sequential ARTP–UV–ARTP Mutagenesis and Mutant Screening

### 1.4.1 Preparation of cell suspensions for mutagenesis

But-6, But-6A100, and But-6B100 were used as the parental strains for the first, second, and third rounds of mutagenesis, respectively. Each parental strain was streaked from a glycerol stock onto an LB agar plate and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. A freshly isolated colony was transferred to a 250 mL Erlenmeyer flask containing 50 mL of liquid LB medium and cultivated at 37 °C and 200 rpm for 10 h. Cells were collected by centrifugation at 6,000 rpm and 4 °C for 5 min. After removal of the supernatant, the pellet was washed three times with sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), resuspended in sterile physiological saline, and counted using a bacterial counting chamber. The cell suspension was adjusted to approximately 1 × 10⁸ CFU/mL for mutagenesis.

### 1.4.2 ARTP and UV mutagenesis

In the first round, But-6 was subjected to ARTP mutagenesis (Fig. 2A). A 10 μL aliquot of the prepared cell suspension was evenly deposited onto the center of a sterile metal carrier slide and exposed in an ARTP mutagenesis apparatus. High-purity nitrogen was specified as the working gas, with a radiofrequency power of 120 W, gas flow rate of 10 standard liters per minute (SLPM), distance between the plasma emitter and sample of 5 mm, and exposure time of 30 s at room temperature. Immediately after treatment, the carrier slide was transferred into a 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube containing 990 μL of sterile liquid seed medium, and the cells were eluted by vortexing for 100 s. The recovered cell suspension was transferred into a 500 mL Erlenmeyer flask containing 50 mL of liquid seed medium and cultured at 37 °C and 200 rpm for 12 h. After serial dilution, 100 μL of the culture was spread onto solid medium and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. Single colonies were selected as candidate mutants.

For the second round, But-6A100, selected from the first-round screen, was subjected to UV mutagenesis. A 5 mL aliquot of the prepared cell suspension was placed in a sterile Petri dish (9 cm diameter) and exposed for 30 s beneath a 30 W UV lamp positioned 30 cm above the sample. The treated suspension was serially diluted and plated under light-protected conditions.

In the third round, the second-round selected strain But-6B100 was subjected to a further ARTP treatment. The exposure settings and subsequent cell recovery and cultivation procedures were the same as those used in the first round.

### 1.4.3 Primary screening and shake-flask validation

Following each round of mutagenesis, 100 candidate mutants were randomly selected from the agar plates and screened in tubes containing liquid fermentation medium. One corresponding parental strain was included as a control in each round and was not counted among the 100 candidate mutants. Butyrate titers were determined after tube cultivation. A candidate with a butyrate titer higher than that of the parental control from the same round was classified as a positive mutant. The highest-producing candidate in each round was selected as the parent for the next mutagenesis round, resulting in the sequential isolation of But-6A100, But-6B100, and But-6C100.

The parental engineered strain But-6 and the three selected mutants were independently evaluated by shake-flask fermentation under the seed-cultivation and fermentation conditions specified in Sections 1.2.3 and 1.2.4. Butyrate titers and OD₆₀₀ were measured at the end of fermentation. Three independent biological replicates were performed for each strain.

## 1.5 Optimization of Fermentation Medium Components

The final mutant But-6C100 was used to evaluate the effects of yeast extract, peptone, corn steep liquor, and molasses concentrations on butyrate production by sequential one-factor-at-a-time experiments using the basal fermentation medium containing 30.0 g/L glucose. The concentration selected for each component was maintained as the basal condition during optimization of the next component. Yeast extract, peptone, and molasses were each tested at 2.5, 5.0, 7.5, and 10.0 g/L, whereas corn steep liquor was tested at 4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, and 12.0 g/L. All treatments were evaluated in shake flasks under the conditions described in Section 1.2.4, and butyrate titers were determined at the end of fermentation. Three independent biological replicates were performed for each treatment.

## 1.6 Fed-Batch Fermentation in a 5 L Bioreactor

Based on the single-factor optimization results, But-6C100 was cultivated in a mechanically stirred 5 L bioreactor using the selected concentrations of yeast extract (7.5 g/L), peptone (5.0 g/L), corn steep liquor (10.0 g/L), and molasses (7.5 g/L) (Fig. 4A). The strain was activated on agar and grown in seed medium before inoculation into the bioreactor at a stated starting OD₆₀₀ of 0.5. The initial working volume was 2.5 L, and the initial glucose concentration was 50.0 g/L. The fermentation temperature was maintained at 37 °C, the agitation rate at 300 rpm, and the aeration rate at 1.0 vvm. The fermentation pH was controlled at 7.0 by automated addition of 4 mol/L NaOH.

When the initial glucose was nearly depleted at 24 h, concentrated glucose solution was continuously supplied using a feeding pump to maintain residual glucose concentrations within a target range of 1–5 g/L. The fermentation lasted 48 h, with aseptic samples collected every 6 h to determine OD₆₀₀, residual glucose concentration, and butyrate titer.

## 1.7 Analytical Methods

OD₆₀₀ was determined by measuring the absorbance of appropriately diluted fermentation broth at 600 nm using a visible-light spectrophotometer. For residual glucose measurements, the supernatant obtained after centrifugation of the fermentation broth was diluted 100-fold and analyzed using an SBA-40D biosensor analyzer.

The concentrations of butyrate, lactate, acetate, and succinate were determined by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). Fermentation broth samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min, and the collected supernatants were filtered through 0.22 μm membrane filters before analysis. Separation was carried out on a Bio-Rad Aminex HPX-87H organic acid analysis column (300 mm × 7.8 mm) with 3.5 mmol/L H₂SO₄ as the mobile phase at a column temperature of 35 °C and a flow rate of 0.6 mL/min. Detection was performed at 210 nm using a UV detector, and the injection volume was 10 μL. Serially diluted standard solutions of butyric, lactic, acetic, and succinic acids were prepared to establish calibration curves from analyte concentration and chromatographic peak area. Organic acid concentrations were quantified using the external standard method.

## 1.8 Data Processing and Statistical Analysis

Three independent biological replicates were performed for shake-flask evaluation of the engineered strains, shake-flask validation of the mutants obtained after sequential mutagenesis, single-factor optimization of fermentation medium components, and the 5 L bioreactor experiment. Data were expressed as the mean ± sample standard deviation (SD). Experimental data plots were generated using Python and exported as scalable vector graphics (SVG). Schematics of the metabolic pathways, mutant-selection process, and fermentation system were created as vector graphics based on the experimental design. Adobe Illustrator was used to standardize the text, line work, and layout of the figures.

The positive mutation rate (%) for each round of primary screening was calculated as the number of candidate mutants with a higher butyrate titer than the corresponding parental control divided by the total number of candidate mutants screened in that round (100), multiplied by 100.
