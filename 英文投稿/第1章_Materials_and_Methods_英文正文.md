# 1 Materials and Methods

## 1.1 Materials

### 1.1.1 Bacterial strains

*Bacillus megaterium* CCTCC M2021167, obtained from the China Center for Type Culture Collection (CCTCC), was used as the starting strain and designated But-0. A series of engineered strains, But-1 through But-6, was sequentially constructed from But-0 by deleting host genes in competing metabolic pathways and integrating heterologous butyrate biosynthetic genes into the corresponding chromosomal loci. Using engineered strain But-6 as the parent, three successive rounds of mutagenesis—atmospheric and room-temperature plasma (ARTP), ultraviolet (UV), and ARTP—were conducted to obtain the mutant strains But-6A100, But-6B100, and But-6C100, respectively. The characteristics and sources of all strains used in this study are listed in Table 1.

### 1.1.2 Main reagents and equipment

Restriction endonucleases, PrimeSTAR high-fidelity DNA polymerase, and Taq DNA polymerase were purchased from TaKaRa Bio (Dalian, China). The one-step homologous recombination kit was obtained from Nanjing Jujiang Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China). Plasmid mini-prep, DNA gel extraction, and PCR product purification kits were purchased from Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China), and the bacterial genomic DNA extraction kit was obtained from Tiangen Biotech (Beijing, China). Heterologous gene synthesis and PCR primer synthesis were performed by Yixin Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). Chromatographic standards of butyric, lactic, acetic, and succinic acids were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA), and all other biochemical and inorganic reagents were obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

The main instruments included a PCR thermal cycler, electroporator, gel imaging system, electrophoresis apparatus, and benchtop high-speed refrigerated centrifuge (Eppendorf, Germany); a visible spectrophotometer (INESA Analytical Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China); an SBA-40D biosensor analyzer (Biology Institute, Shandong Academy of Sciences, China); a high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA); an analytical balance and precision pH meter (Mettler-Toledo, Switzerland); a 5 L stirred-tank bioreactor (T&J Bio-engineering (Shanghai) Co., Ltd., China); an ARTP mutagenesis breeding system (Wuxi Yuanqing Tianmu Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China); and a UV mutagenesis apparatus.

## 1.2 Culture Media and Cultivation Conditions

### 1.2.1 Seed media

The solid seed medium used for strain activation contained (g/L): glucose, 30.0; yeast extract, 5.0; peptone, 5.0; and agar, 15.0. The liquid seed medium contained (g/L): glucose, 30.0; yeast extract, 5.0; and peptone, 5.0. Both media were prepared at natural pH and autoclaved at 121 °C for 20 min (glucose was prepared and autoclaved separately, then aseptically mixed with the other components after cooling prior to inoculation). Luria–Bertani (LB) medium used for plasmid construction and preparation of mutagenized cell suspensions contained (g/L): peptone, 10.0; yeast extract, 5.0; and NaCl, 10.0 (with 15.0 g/L agar added for solid LB medium).

### 1.2.2 Fermentation medium

The basal liquid fermentation medium contained (g/L): glucose, 30.0; yeast extract, 5.0; and peptone, 5.0. The medium was used at its natural pH and autoclaved at 121 °C for 20 min, with glucose sterilized separately and aseptically mixed with the other components before use.

### 1.2.3 Seed cultivation

Preserved strains were streaked onto solid activation medium and incubated at 30 °C for 24 h. A single colony was inoculated into a 100 mL Erlenmeyer flask containing 25 mL of liquid seed medium and cultivated at 30 °C and 200 rpm for 16 h until reaching the logarithmic growth phase to serve as the seed culture.

### 1.2.4 Shake-flask fermentation

The seed culture was inoculated at 1% (v/v) into a 500 mL Erlenmeyer flask containing 100 mL of fermentation medium and cultivated at 30 °C and 200 rpm for 72 h. At the end of fermentation, samples were taken to measure the optical density at 600 nm ($\text{OD}_{600}$) and the concentrations of butyrate, lactate, acetate, and succinate in the fermentation broth.

## 1.3 Construction of Engineered Strains

### 1.3.1 Preparation of homologous recombination donor DNA fragments

Homologous recombination donor DNA fragments were constructed according to the target loci and integrated genes listed in Table 1. Taking the construction of engineered strain But-1 as an example, the upstream and downstream homology arms of the *ldhA* locus were amplified by PCR using the genomic DNA of the starting strain But-0 as the template, and the target gene fragment was amplified from a template carrying the *E. coli*-derived *atoB*$^{\mathrm{Ec}}$ gene. After ligating the target gene with expression regulatory elements including a strong promoter and a terminator, the upstream homology arm, the *atoB*$^{\mathrm{Ec}}$ expression cassette, and the downstream homology arm were assembled into a complete homologous recombination donor DNA fragment via overlap-extension PCR (fusion PCR). Donor DNA fragments for the remaining target loci were prepared using the same method.

### 1.3.2 Sequential construction of engineered strains

Chromosomal gene modifications were performed using CRISPR-Cas9-mediated homologous recombination. The constructed donor DNA fragment was co-electroporated into competent host cells along with the pTargetF plasmid (carrying the sgRNA targeting the specific locus) and the pCas plasmid to achieve homologous recombination repair at the target site. Starting from But-0, the *ldhA* gene was deleted and replaced with *atoB*$^{\mathrm{Ec}}$ at the same locus to obtain strain But-1; subsequently, using But-1 as the parent, the *ackA* gene was deleted and replaced with *hbd*$^{\mathrm{Ca}}$ at the same locus ($\Delta ackA::hbd^{\mathrm{Ca}}$) to obtain strain But-2. Following the same procedure, the modifications $\Delta adhE::crt^{\mathrm{Ca}}$, $\Delta pflB::ter^{\mathrm{Ca}}$, and $\Delta frdABCD::tesB^{\mathrm{Ec}}$ were sequentially introduced to generate engineered strains But-3, But-4, and But-5, respectively. Finally, based on strain But-5, five additional gene deletion-and-integration modifications—$\Delta poxB::atoB^{\mathrm{Ec}}$, $\Delta pta::hbd^{\mathrm{Ca}}$, $\Delta alaT::crt^{\mathrm{Ca}}$, $\Delta ppc::ter^{\mathrm{Td}}$, and $\Delta mgsA::tesB^{\mathrm{Ec}}$—were sequentially completed to obtain engineered strain But-6.

### 1.3.3 Screening and verification of recombinant strains

Following electroporation and recovery, cell suspensions were spread onto selective agar plates containing the appropriate antibiotics. Single colonies were picked as candidate transformants and screened by colony PCR using verification primers located outside the homology arms of the target locus, combined with agarose gel electrophoresis and DNA sequencing to confirm gene deletion and heterologous gene integration. Verified positive clones were subjected to induction and cultivation at an elevated temperature to cure the editing plasmids pTargetF and pCas. After re-verifying the target locus sequence, the strains were preserved in glycerol stocks for the next round of genetic modification.

## 1.4 Sequential ARTP–UV–ARTP Mutagenesis and Strain Screening

### 1.4.1 Preparation of cell suspensions for mutagenesis

Strains But-6, But-6A100, and But-6B100 served as the parental strains for the three successive rounds of mutagenesis, respectively. Each parental strain was streaked from a glycerol stock onto an LB agar plate and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. A fresh single colony was inoculated into a 250 mL Erlenmeyer flask containing 50 mL of liquid LB medium and cultured at 37 °C and 200 rpm for 10 h. The culture was centrifuged at 6,000 rpm and 4 °C for 5 min. After discarding the supernatant, the cell pellet was washed three times with sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), resuspended in sterile physiological saline, and counted using a bacterial counting chamber to adjust the cell concentration to approximately $1 \times 10^8$ CFU/mL prior to use.

### 1.4.2 ARTP and UV mutagenesis procedures

In the first round, starting strain But-6 was subjected to ARTP mutagenesis (Fig. 2A). A 10 μL aliquot of the prepared cell suspension was evenly spotted onto the center of a sterilized metal slide and placed in the ARTP mutagenesis apparatus. High-purity nitrogen was used as the working gas, with the radiofrequency power set at 120 W, treatment temperature at room temperature, gas flow rate at 10 SLPM, distance between the plasma emitter and sample at 5 mm, and exposure time of 30 s. Immediately after treatment, the metal slide was transferred into a 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube containing 990 μL of sterile liquid seed medium and vortexed for 100 s to elute the cells. The eluate was then transferred into a 500 mL shake flask containing 50 mL of liquid seed medium and incubated at 37 °C and 200 rpm for 12 h. After appropriate serial dilution, 100 μL of the culture was spread onto solid medium plates and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h, from which single colonies were picked as candidate mutants. In the second round, the high-yielding mutant But-6A100 selected from the first round was used as the parent for UV mutagenesis: 5 mL of the prepared cell suspension was placed in a sterile Petri dish (9 cm diameter) and irradiated for 30 s directly beneath a 30 W UV lamp at a distance of 30 cm; the treated suspension was serially diluted and plated in the dark throughout the process. In the third round, the selected mutant But-6B100 from the second round served as the parent for the second ARTP mutagenesis, following the same treatment parameters and cell recovery procedures as the first ARTP round.

### 1.4.3 Primary screening and shake-flask validation

After each round of mutagenesis, 100 candidate mutants were randomly picked from the plates (alongside one corresponding parental strain as a control) and inoculated into test tubes containing liquid fermentation medium for primary screening to determine butyrate titers. Candidates exhibiting a higher butyrate titer than the corresponding parental control were defined as positive mutants. The highest-producing mutants from the first two rounds, But-6A100 and But-6B100, served as the parents for the subsequent rounds, while But-6C100 was retained as the final selected mutant.

The starting strain But-6 and the three selected mutants (But-6A100, But-6B100, and But-6C100) were independently validated by shake-flask fermentation following the procedures described in Sections 1.2.3 and 1.2.4. Butyrate titers and $\text{OD}_{600}$ were measured at the end of fermentation, with three independent biological replicates performed for each strain.

## 1.5 Optimization of Fermentation Medium Components

Using mutant strain But-6C100 as the test strain and the basal fermentation medium (containing 30.0 g/L glucose) as the starting point, sequential single-factor experiments were conducted to investigate the effects of varying concentrations of yeast extract, peptone, corn steep liquor, and molasses on butyrate production (the optimal concentration determined for each preceding component was maintained as the baseline condition for the next component). Yeast extract, peptone, and molasses were each tested at four concentration gradients (2.5, 5.0, 7.5, and 10.0 g/L), whereas corn steep liquor was tested at five concentration gradients (4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, and 12.0 g/L). All treatment groups were cultivated in shake flasks as described in Section 1.2.4, and butyrate titers were measured at the end of fermentation, with three biological replicates per treatment.

## 1.6 Fed-Batch Fermentation in a 5 L Bioreactor

Based on the single-factor optimization results, fed-batch fermentation of mutant strain But-6C100 was carried out in a 5 L stirred-tank bioreactor using the optimized medium components (7.5 g/L yeast extract, 5.0 g/L peptone, 10.0 g/L corn steep liquor, and 7.5 g/L molasses) (Fig. 4A). Following plate activation and seed cultivation, the culture was inoculated into the 5 L bioreactor at an initial $\text{OD}_{600}$ of 0.5. The working volume of the bioreactor was 2.5 L with an initial glucose concentration of 50.0 g/L, and cultivation was conducted at 37 °C with an agitation speed of 300 rpm and an aeration rate of 1.0 vvm. During fermentation, the broth pH was maintained at 7.0 by automated feeding of 4 mol/L NaOH solution. When the initial glucose was nearly exhausted at 24 h, a concentrated glucose solution was continuously fed using a feeding pump to maintain the residual glucose concentration within the target feeding window of 1–5 g/L. The fermentation lasted for 48 h, during which samples were aseptically withdrawn every 6 h to determine $\text{OD}_{600}$, residual glucose concentration, and butyrate titer.

## 1.7 Analytical Methods

Determination of $\text{OD}_{600}$: Fermentation broth was appropriately diluted, and the absorbance was measured at 600 nm using a visible spectrophotometer. Determination of residual glucose concentration: The supernatant obtained by centrifuging the fermentation broth was diluted 100-fold and analyzed using an SBA-40D biosensor analyzer.

Determination of organic acid concentrations: The concentrations of butyrate, lactate, acetate, and succinate in the fermentation broth were determined by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). Fermentation broth samples were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min, and the collected supernatants were filtered through a 0.22 μm microporous membrane prior to injection. Chromatographic conditions were as follows: a Bio-Rad Aminex HPX-87H organic acid analysis column (300 mm × 7.8 mm); mobile phase of 3.5 mmol/L $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$; column temperature of 35 °C; flow rate of 0.6 mL/min; UV detector wavelength of 210 nm; and injection volume of 10 μL. Serial gradient standard solutions of butyric, lactic, acetic, and succinic acids were prepared to construct calibration curves by plotting mass concentration against the corresponding chromatographic peak area, and each organic acid was quantified using the external standard method.

## 1.8 Data Processing and Statistical Analysis

Three independent biological replicates were set up for shake-flask evaluation of engineered strains, shake-flask validation of representative mutants from sequential mutagenesis, single-factor optimization of medium components, and 5 L bioreactor scale-up experiments; experimental data are expressed as the mean ± sample standard deviation (mean ± SD). Experimental data plots were generated using Python and exported in scalable vector graphics (SVG) format. Schematic diagrams of the metabolic pathway, strain breeding workflow, and bioreactor setup were drawn as vector graphics based on the experimental design, and Adobe Illustrator was used to uniformly adjust the text, lines, and layout of the figures. The positive mutation rate (%) was calculated as the percentage of mutants exhibiting a higher butyrate titer than the corresponding parental control in primary screening relative to the total number of candidate mutants screened in that round (100 strains).
